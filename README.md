# Group Theme: The Business of College Sports

Our group wants to study how money moves through college sports. We will look at where athletic departments get their money, what they spend it on, what happens to an athlete’s financial value after college and how smaller schools get paid to play bigger programs. We also want to show why one total number does not always tell the full story. Schools can report similar revenue while making and spending their money in different ways. Our main focus will be college sports finances, NIL, athlete outcomes and payments between schools. Fan culture, normal sports statistics and unrelated professional sports will be outside our scope.

## Scholarly Context

Research on college sports finances shows why we need to be careful when comparing numbers from different sources. Jones studied EADA financial data and found concerns with the consistency and reliability of some of the reported numbers (Jones, 2020). This connects to Brayden’s project because he is comparing EADA with Knight-Newhouse and the Big Ten’s Form 990 instead of depending on only one source.

Lawrence, Gabriel and Tuttle found that athletic departments do not always organize and report expenses in the same way (Lawrence, Gabriel, & Tuttle, 2010). This matters for both Brayden and Abhinav because we are comparing financial categories across different schools and databases.

Penrose explains that NIL market value is difficult to define because it depends on how the value is calculated and what information is available (Penrose, 2023). This relates to Vedant because an On3 NIL valuation should not be treated as the exact amount of money an athlete earned.

Ballou and Nordstrom studied guarantee games between FCS and FBS schools and looked at the risks for athletes on the smaller teams (Ballou & Nordstrom, 2020). This connects to Chinglin’s project because it shows that guarantee games involve more than the payout and final score.
